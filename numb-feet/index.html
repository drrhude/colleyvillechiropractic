What Is Numbness + Tingling in Feet?

Numbness, tingling or burning in the feet usually comes from irritated or damaged nerves. The two most common sources are peripheral neuropathy, where the small nerves in the feet are affected, and a pinched nerve in the lower back, like sciatica or spinal stenosis. Age, vitamin B12 deficiency, alcohol, thyroid problems and some medications can also play a role.

The treatment depends on the source. Back-related numbness often responds to chiropractic care and rehab. Neuropathy in both feet usually fits our NeuroPath program. And some causes, like B12 or thyroid problems, need bloodwork with your doctor, so we'll tell you when that's the next step.

If no clear cause is found, it's called idiopathic neuropathy. It's common in adults over 60, and NeuroPath can still help with symptoms and balance.

Common Questions

Have another question? Call or text (817) 420-7696.

Is numbness in my feet from my back?

It can be. A pinched nerve in the lower back can cause numbness or tingling down the leg and into the foot, usually on one side. Neuropathy more often affects both feet evenly. Testing tells us which.

When should I worry about numb feet?

Sudden numbness, weakness, numbness in the groin, or new bladder or bowel changes need emergency care. Gradual numbness should still be checked, sooner rather than later.

Do you check vitamin B12 levels?

We don't draw blood in the clinic. If your history points to B12, thyroid or blood sugar, we'll recommend bloodwork with your doctor.
