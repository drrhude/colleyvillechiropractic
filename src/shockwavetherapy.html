How it works

Radial shockwave sends quick pressure waves into the painful tissue through a handheld applicator. It's most often used for long-standing tendon problems, where the tissue has stopped healing the way it should.

Research supports shockwave for several tendon conditions, especially plantar fasciitis, tennis elbow and calcific shoulder tendinopathy. It works best alongside the right exercise, so most plans include both.

  • Sessions take about 15 minutes
  • Expect some tenderness during treatment; we adjust the intensity to you
  • Most plans run a few weekly sessions, then we reassess

What we use it for

Shockwave is a fit for specific problems. Your exam tells us whether it's the right tool for yours.

  • Plantar fasciitis
  • Heel spurs
  • Achilles tendinopathy
  • Tennis elbow
  • Golfer's elbow
  • Calcific shoulder tendinopathy
  • Rotator cuff tendinopathy
  • Patellar tendinopathy
  • Hip tendon pain
  • Shin splints
  • Stubborn muscle trigger points

When it's not the right fit

Shockwave isn't for everyone. We screen for these before your first session:

  • Pregnancy
  • Bleeding disorders or blood thinners
  • A pacemaker or other implanted device near the area
  • Active infection, open wounds or cancer in the treatment area
  • A recent PRP injection: we wait 2 weeks with clearance from your injecting physician, or 4 weeks without
