Movement Is Medicine

If you remember one thing from your time with us a year from now, we want it to be this. Your body is built to heal and get strong. Our job is to show it the way back.

Pain Is Always Real

We never debate whether your pain is real. The question is where it's coming from: damaged tissue, overworked tissue, or a nervous system that has become extra sensitive. Usually it's a mix, and each one feeds the others.

Finding that source is the first job of your care. Everything else is built on it.

"We don't have weak bodies. We have weak systems that need improvement."

Dr. Jon "Rudy" Urrutia, DC

What We Believe

The principles behind every care plan we build, in plain English.

There's Rarely One Cause

Back pain seldom comes from one moment. Load builds up over months while life gets busier and we get less active. Then one ordinary movement becomes the straw that broke the camel's back.

Your Alarm System Learns

Every flare-up teaches your nervous system to protect you sooner. Over time it gets more sensitive, so it hurts more easily, even when the tissue could handle the activity.

Rest Is the Trap

Rest has its place, but movement is what improves pain. Guarding costs mobility, and lost mobility forces other areas to pick up the slack until they start to hurt too.

No Cookie-Cutter Plans

We start with one question: what can't you do right now? Then we build the fastest path back to those things. Two people with the same diagnosis rarely get the same plan.

Push Hard, Intelligently

We train at about a 7 to 8 out of 10 effort. Easier than that doesn't change anything; harder risks irritating the tissue. Exercise effort is fine. Pain pain means we stop and adjust.

Strength Turns the Alarm Down

Muscles, joints and nerves work as one system. A weak muscle guards itself and pulls on joints. Strength lets muscles absorb force and tells your nervous system it's safe, which raises your pain threshold.

Three Phases of Recovery

Most care plans follow the same arc. How long each phase lasts depends on you.

Usually 2 to 4 weeks

Get Out of Pain

Find the source and calm it down fast with adjustments, shockwave, soft-tissue work and gentle movement.

Next

Move Well Again

Rebuild clean movement patterns so your body stops compensating and the load gets shared the right way.

About the final 4 weeks

Reinforce It

Strengthen the tissue and remind your body it's okay to be asked to do hard things. This is the phase most people skip, and why pain comes back.

The 35 / 65 Rule

What we do in the office is only part of the picture. The rest of your week matters more.

35%In our office
65%Your life between visits

In the office: adjustments, treatment and coached rehab. The spark that gets things moving.

Outside it: sleep, stress, activity and how you think about your pain. Sleep is when your body rebuilds. Stress keeps your nervous system on high alert and wrecks sleep. Confidence matters too: if your nervous system believes you're hurt, you'll feel it.

Exercise Pain vs. Pain Pain

We'll ask you to work hard, and some discomfort is part of getting stronger. Here's how to tell the difference.

Green: GoMuscle burn, effort, fatigue. This is exercise pain, and it's how you get stronger.
Yellow: AdjustFamiliar pain that settles quickly. Slow down, lighten the load, or shorten the range.
Red: StopSharp, worsening or radiating pain. Stop that movement, note it, and we'll modify it.

Care Doesn't End When Pain Does

The most common story we hear: someone did therapy, felt better, stopped, and the pain came back. Care ended, but life stayed the same. That's why we finish with a reinforcement phase and keep you moving after, in the clinic with a membership or online with RESET.
