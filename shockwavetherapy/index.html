What Is Shockwave Therapy?

Shockwave therapy, also called ESWT, uses a handpiece to deliver acoustic pressure waves into a painful tendon or soft tissue. The controlled stimulation increases blood flow and triggers a healing response in tissue that has stopped healing on its own. A typical plan is 5 to 10 sessions, once a week.

Shockwave works best alongside the right tendon-loading exercises, so most plans include both. We use a radial shockwave device, which spreads energy over a wider area and is well suited to tendon and muscle problems close to the surface.

What to Expect

  • A few minutes per area; intense but tolerable, and we adjust it to you
  • Mild soreness, redness or tenderness for 24 to 48 hours is normal
  • Change builds gradually over the series, not after one session

After Your Visit

  • Skip anti-inflammatories (ibuprofen, Advil, Aleve, naproxen, aspirin) around treatment days
  • Use acetaminophen if you need pain relief; confirm with your provider or pharmacist
  • No ice on the treated area. Heat and gentle movement are fine
  • Keep up your tendon-loading exercises

Is It Right for You?

Tell us before treatment if any of these apply:

  • Pregnancy
  • Bleeding disorders or blood thinners
  • A pacemaker or implanted device near the area
  • Cancer, infection or open wounds at the treatment site
  • A recent steroid or PRP injection: after PRP we wait 2 weeks with clearance from your injecting physician, or 4 weeks without
What the research says. Shockwave has strong support from systematic reviews and randomized trials for tendinopathies and plantar fasciitis. We avoid NSAIDs and ice because shockwave works by triggering a controlled healing response, and both blunt that process.

Common Questions

Have another question? Call or text (817) 420-7696.

Does shockwave therapy hurt?

It can feel intense, but it's tolerable, and we adjust the intensity to you. Each area takes only a few minutes. Mild soreness for a day or two afterward is normal.

How many shockwave sessions will I need?

Most plans are 5 to 10 sessions, once a week. Plantar fasciitis and most tendon problems fall in that range. We reassess as we go.

Why can't I take ibuprofen after shockwave?

Shockwave triggers a controlled healing response. Anti-inflammatory drugs and ice suppress that same process and can reduce your results. Acetaminophen is usually fine; check with your provider or pharmacist.

How much does shockwave therapy cost?

A single session is $149. Packages bring it down to $110 per session (5-pack) or $99 per session (10-pack), and members pay $79 per session.
