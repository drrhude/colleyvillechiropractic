What Is Therapeutic Ultrasound?

Therapeutic ultrasound uses a wand that glides over the skin with gel, sending sound waves that warm muscle and soft tissue below the surface. It's used to ease stiffness and prepare tissue for stretching and exercise, and it's included as one of your therapies with each chiropractic visit.

Ultrasound sets the stage for active care. It's most useful as part of a fuller plan, not on its own.

Reviewed by Dr. Jon "Rudy" Urrutia, DC
Updated October 2026

What It Helps With

Your exam tells us whether ultrasound is the right fit for you.

  • Muscle tightness and spasm
  • Soft-tissue stiffness
  • Subacute soft-tissue irritation
  • Trigger points
  • Pre-exercise tissue prep

What to Expect

  • Mild, pleasant warmth or not much at all
  • Comfortable, with no soreness
  • Stretching or exercise right after, while tissue is warm

After Your Visit

  • Do your mobility work while the tissue is warm
  • Stay hydrated
  • Apply heat at home if it helped

Is It Right for You?

Tell us before treatment if any of these apply:

  • An implanted device
  • Pregnancy or possible pregnancy
  • A clotting disorder
What the research says. Therapeutic ultrasound is best viewed as an adjunct that may reduce stiffness and prepare tissue. It works best combined with active exercise and manual care.

Is therapeutic ultrasound the same as an imaging ultrasound?

No. It uses sound waves to warm tissue for treatment, not to take pictures.
