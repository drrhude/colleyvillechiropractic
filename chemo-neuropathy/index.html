What Is Chemotherapy-Induced Neuropathy?

Chemotherapy-induced peripheral neuropathy (CIPN) is nerve damage caused by certain cancer drugs. It often affects both the hands and feet, causing numbness, tingling, burning or trouble with fine tasks like buttoning a shirt, and it can last months or years after treatment ends.

We work with survivors who have finished treatment, and we contact your oncology team before starting anything. NeuroPath uses gentle home light therapy and nerve stimulation, hand and foot dexterity work, and balance and strength training, with re-testing along the way.

Common Symptoms

  • Loss of balance
  • Sensitivity to cold

What to Expect

  • A history that includes your cancer treatment and current medications
  • Sensation, grip and balance testing
  • A plan shared with your oncology team

After Your Visit

  • Use your home devices as directed
  • Keep up your hand and balance exercises
  • Report any new symptoms to your oncology team

Is It Right for You?

Tell us before treatment if any of these apply:

  • Active cancer treatment: we coordinate with your oncologist before starting
  • Light therapy is never applied over a known tumor site
  • A pacemaker or implanted electronic device
  • Lymphedema, which changes how we treat the affected limb
What the research says. Exercise, including balance and strength training, has growing support for easing CIPN symptoms and improving function. Evidence for light therapy and electrical stimulation in CIPN is early and mixed. We measure your sensation, grip and balance so the plan follows your results.

Common Questions

Have another question? Call or text (817) 420-7696.

Can I start NeuroPath during chemotherapy?

We generally start after active treatment, and only with your oncology team's okay. Call us and we'll talk through your timeline.

Will chemo neuropathy go away on its own?

For some people it fades over months. For others it lingers for years. Testing gives us a baseline so you can see whether it's changing.

Do you talk to my oncologist?

Yes. With your permission, we share our findings and plan with your oncology team before we start.
